## Electrical standards Phases 5–6 (current)

The eighth Electrical Studio tab now links findings to the diagram and freezes immutable, downloadable compliance revisions. `src/lib/electrical/design-issues.ts` merges the electrical `designReview` and the standards `evaluateCompliance` output into `LinkedIssue`, each carrying `DiagramTarget` references (`board` / `circuit` / `floor`). Only defensible mappings link: phase balance and demand basis → the main board, over-limit voltage-drop rows → their circuits, uncovered floor-plan points → their floor. Everything without a single element is reported as **project scope** rather than guessed onto a board; issues are sorted most-severe first. The Distribution view adds an "Issues linked to the distribution" section whose chips anchor to the single-line board boxes, riser floors and board-schedule rows (`#board-<id>`, `#circuit-<id>`, `#floor-<id>`). A link identifies where an issue sits; it is not an automatic fix.

`src/lib/engineering/compliance/revisions.ts` adds immutable revisions and stale-report detection. `complianceFingerprint` hashes the evaluation-relevant inputs (service, cable assumptions, compliance context, load schedule, registry/engine versions) with a non-cryptographic FNV-1a 64-bit content fingerprint; `complianceInputSignature` derives the canonical signature. `createComplianceRevision` freezes an append-only `ComplianceRevision` snapshot (revision number, author, ISO timestamp, fingerprint, registry/engine versions, status counts, checks, applicability matrix, calculation evidence, disclaimer), and `isRevisionStale` compares the current project fingerprint against the frozen one — a later edit marks the revision stale rather than changing it. `compliance-report.ts` renders a **white, print-friendly** A4 PDF (dark text, thin rules) reproducing the frozen revision with a prominent disclaimer and a `CURRENT` / `STALE` / `NOT CHECKED` staleness line, served by `POST /api/reports/compliance` (per-instance rate guard of 30/min, 200 KB body cap, JSON only, schema-validated). The Standards & Compliance tab adds a "Compliance revisions & reports" panel (create, list, stale badge, download) mounted in `standards-compliance.tsx`; revisions persist to browser localStorage only (`caldriva-compliance-revisions-v1`).

Verification for this slice: lint (0 errors) and TypeScript passed; the production build generated 46 routes; 213 unit tests across 31 files passed (adding diagram-link, revision, fingerprint/staleness and PDF-rendering coverage); 76 desktop/mobile browser checks passed, including a new end-to-end check that records a revision, detects staleness after a project edit, and downloads the print-friendly PDF. The fingerprint is a software content-integrity check, not a digital signature; the report is not a certificate; reviewer status remains UNREVIEWED. Cloud-hosted revision storage, signed reviewer decisions and edit/delete auditing remain for Phase 8.

## Auto Electrical Design Studio slice (current)

An automated multistorey-building electrical design module is implemented inside the existing platform at `/tools/electrical` (replacing the previous "Planned module" placeholder). It is a deterministic, Zod-validated domain module (`src/lib/electrical/`) with a client studio (`src/components/electrical/`). A structured **brief** (name, country, jurisdiction scope, category, basement/ground/upper floors, apartments per floor, apartment template, and common services) generates an editable **hierarchy**: floors → apartments → rooms → loads, using T0–T5/CUSTOM apartment templates and a room/appliance catalogue (`APPLIANCES`) with explicit watts, power factor, phases, utilisation and demand factors. Every id is derived deterministically (`floorCode`, `uniqueId`).

Loads are aggregated into connected W/VA, utilisation- and demand-adjusted demand, and indicative energy (`src/lib/electrical/loads.ts`), with per-phase (L1/L2/L3) distribution and imbalance from explicit, editable apartment/building diversity and design-margin factors. Electrical and common appliances are grouped into circuits (LGT, SOC, KITCHEN, HVAC, DED, EV, LIFT, PUMP, TECH) and assembled into a distribution topology of MDB → floor board (FDB) → apartment board (ADB) plus a common services board (CSB), with a single-phase balancing pass and generated single-line and riser diagrams (`circuits.ts`). A review engine (`review.ts`) reports pass/check/blocked/manual findings across missing data, loading, circuit completeness, phase balance, protection, earthing, voltage drop, safety zones and regulatory review, and never auto-approves a design.

The studio offers a brief wizard, an editable hierarchy, a floor-plan editor with an auto point generator, a services panel, a load summary, the distribution/diagram view and the review view, with browser-local draft persistence (`caldriva-electrical-v1`). The floor-plan editor (`geometry.ts`/`plan.ts`, `src/components/electrical/plan-editor.tsx`) draws rooms as rectangles in metres on a per-floor plan, links each placed room to a hierarchy room, and proposes **electrical points** (lighting, socket, switch, dedicated) deterministically from room type and area — socket outlets every ~3.5 m of wall with per-type minimums, ceiling lights sized by area. Points are editable (drag, numeric entry, add/delete) and can be synced back into the load model (`syncLoadsFromPlan` sets the linked room's lighting/socket quantities and area). A point→circuit scheduling engine (`schedule.ts`) writes circuit ids back onto loads (`assignCircuitIds`), traces each point to the apartment circuit of the matching type (lighting→LGT, socket→SOC, dedicated→KITCHEN/DED, HVAC→HVAC; switches and data outlets are control/information devices with no circuit), and produces a **Point schedule** and coverage statistics shown in the distribution view. Gaps (placed points with no circuit) raise a review warning. A cable-route/voltage-drop engine (`routes.ts`) auto-sketches L-shaped Manhattan cable routes from the board anchor (a `db` point, else the floor origin) to each scheduled point, measures worst-branch and total lengths, and estimates voltage drop from explicit, editable cable assumptions (material, per-type cross-section, drop target). The distribution view exposes these assumptions and a per-circuit drop table, and the review engine replaces the previous manual voltage-drop check with a computed preliminary estimate. Coordinates are metres relative to the floor origin and are **not** georeferenced; routes are simplified sketches, not installed paths. Cloud persistence is owner-only via migration `202610100005_electrical_designs.sql` (`electrical_designs` + `electrical_drafts`, deny-by-default RLS, `save_electrical_design` RPC with optimistic version checks); `/dashboard/electrical` lists cloud designs. DWG/IFC/vector-PDF import, real route tracing, verified cable/protective-device selection, BOQ and regulatory compliance are **not** implemented; outputs are preliminary and not construction-ready.

The rectangle CAD editor adds Shift-click and marquee room selection, grouped movement, a bounded 50-entry session history (one drag is one undo), room geometry copy/paste/duplicate, grid/object snapping and alignment guides, width/height dimension lines, numeric geometry editing, fixed-size room locks, and one-shot alignment/equal-size commands. Zoom uses the SVG viewBox so pointer coordinates remain correct after zoom/pan; controls include buttons, a Pan tool, Ctrl/Cmd+wheel and two-pointer pinch. Architectural annotations (wall thickness and door/window edge, width and position) persist in the room schema, with optional fields for older drafts. Room transforms carry attached electrical points; moving geometry clears routes until regenerated. History is session-local; alignment/equal-size commands are not persistent parametric relationships.

SVG/PNG exports embed resolved theme colours and capture the full plan, with resize handles removed. PNG dimensions are capped at 4096 pixels. DXF export (`cad-export.ts`) writes metre-unit room outlines, point circles on kind-specific layers, and cable-route segments with Y converted to CAD's upward axis. It excludes wall thickness, openings and dimension annotations; it is a geometry export, not a construction drawing or DWG conversion. Clipboard copies room geometry only and detaches hierarchy links. No CAD import has been added.

Verification: lint and TypeScript passed, the production build generated 42 routes, 124 unit/PostgreSQL tests passed (18 files), and all 54 desktop/mobile browser checks passed. Regression coverage includes point transforms and route invalidation, saved architectural properties, DXF units/coordinates, browser undo/redo, locked dimensions, opening persistence, all three downloads, and grouped movement/undo at zoomed scale. The PGlite suite executes migrations `0001`–`0005`. Hosted Supabase, CAD import and installed cable routes remain unverified.

## Private file storage slice (current)

Owner-scoped building file uploads are implemented on top of the persistence slice. `supabase/migrations/202610100004_building_files.sql` creates `building_files` (owner, building, storage path, name, MIME, size ≤10 MB, SHA-256) with deny-by-default RLS and a security-definer `register_building_file` RPC that fixes the storage path to `<owner>/<building>/<file>`. The Supabase Storage bucket `building-files` and its `storage.objects` policies are provisioned separately (`supabase/storage/building_files.sql`) and are not PGlite-testable. Server actions (`files-actions.ts`) handle listing, upload (multipart FormData, SHA-256 via Web Crypto, rollback on failure), signed download URL (`createSignedUrl` ~300s) and delete (bucket + metadata). `next.config.ts` raises `serverActions.bodySizeLimit` to 12 MB for multipart overhead. `/dashboard/buildings/[id]/files` with client `BuildingFiles` component lists, uploads, downloads and removes files; each cloud building card now links to its file workspace. Verification: lint, TypeScript, production build, 49 unit/PostgreSQL tests and 24 desktop/mobile browser checks passed. PGlite suite executes migrations `0001`–`0004`, covering registration, path derivation, cross-owner denial, stale rejection, owner delete and anonymous denial. Hosted storage is not live-verified.

Customers submit engineering requests from `/request-quote`; signed-in submissions persist to their account and appear at `/dashboard/quotes`. Staff with the `engineer` or `admin` role work the queue at `/dashboard/engineer`: claim an open request, compose a multi-line quotation (description, quantity, unit, unit price) with tax, currency, notes and validity, save drafts and send. Customers accept or decline the latest sent quotation; declining returns the request to the engineer for a revision, which is stored as a new immutable version and supersedes the earlier one.

Migration `202610100003_quotation_workflow.sql` adds `profiles` (role registry), `quotation_requests`, `quotations` and `quotation_items`. All four tables are deny-by-default with RLS enabled and SELECT-only grants to clients; every mutation runs through a security-definer function that re-checks `auth.uid()`, role and state. Role rules: `bootstrap_admin` seeds the first administrator using database-owner privileges, `set_user_role` lets only admins change roles (never their own, and never removing the last admin), and `current_user_role` backs the visibility policies. Request lifecycle: `new → assigned → quoted → accepted → completed` with `withdrawn`/`rejected` side exits, enforced by `submit_quotation_request`, `cancel_quotation_request`, `claim_quotation_request`, `assign_quotation_request`, `save_quotation`, `respond_to_quotation` and `transition_quotation_request`.

## Architecture
- App Router server pages for public content; client island for Building Studio.
- Independent Zod-validated SI calculation modules, reused by server route handlers.
- Server-side PVGIS adapter: fixed upstream origin, bounded parameters, timeout, validated responses; never substitute synthetic yields on failure.
- Leaflet map with attributed OSM tiles. User-drawn geometry, manual inclination/azimuth; estimates explicitly distinguished from measurements.
- Browser-local building drafts plus optional Supabase authentication and owner-scoped cloud draft persistence. Simulation results are still session-local.
- UI: brand configuration → semantic CSS → accessible primitives → layout → discipline pages / studio → results.

## Database design (planned, not deployed)
Supabase auth.users owns profiles; organizations have memberships and roles. Buildings belong to an owner/organization, with building_floors, roof_planes, building_geometry and private building_files. Electrical designs persist as `electrical_designs`/`electrical_drafts` (owner-only jsonb draft, migration `202610100005`). Each engineering input stores value, unit, provenance, timestamp and source. Simulations reference immutable calculation_versions and separate simulation_inputs/results snapshots. Projects reference quotation_requests, quotations/items, assignments, deliverables/revisions, messages and audit_logs. Engineers/qualifications are reviewed separately from user-editable profiles. Service_pricing is admin-only. Index owner_id, project_id and foreign keys. Enable deny-by-default RLS on every table; ownership and verified assignment policies must be tested before cloud release. Private storage access uses authorized signed URLs. Admin roles cannot be self-assigned.

## Roadmap and boundaries
1. Correct foundation and truthful public catalogue.
2. Local building model, map and roof editing; geocoder requires provider setup.
3. Minimum solar estimation using real PVGIS response and transparent annual financial assumptions.
4. Supabase authentication, RLS migrations/tests, cloud drafts, quotation ingestion and private files.
5. Reviewed professional workflow, then further disciplines in the requested order.

Steps 1–4 are largely implemented: authentication, owner-scoped cloud drafts and solar simulations, the quotation workflow with staff roles, and private building file storage are done and tested in PGlite. Organisation membership remains. Hosted live verification is still required (see `docs/LIVE_VERIFICATION.md`).

## External requirements and limits
PVGIS endpoint availability and geographical coverage constrain solar estimates. Review JRC terms and service quotas before deployment. OpenStreetMap standard tile usage must comply with its attribution/usage policy; configure a commercial tile provider for production load. Address geocoding needs a licensed provider and server credentials. No aerial imagery or roof pitch is inferred. Leaflet, Turf, Radix and the existing dependencies require a license/dependency review before release. Browser storage is not account storage or a backup. Preliminary solar and compliance PDFs exist, but hourly self-consumption, shading, battery dispatch, certified designs and cloud workflows remain explicit follow-up work. Anonymous API abuse protection needs a shared rate limiter at the deployment edge.
